His mood drifts on its own and reacts to how you treat him. Pick one to see it.

HappyWhen you pet him or say something nice.

    He's not an assistant. He's a buddy.

    He hangs out on your screen while you do your thing. Talk to him whenever you want — or ignore him, and see what happens.

    • Just talk to him

      He listens on his own — no buttons — and answers out loud. Cut him off mid-sentence and he'll let you know he noticed.

    • He has moods

      Chill, happy, grumpy, tired, sarcastic. Be nice and he's sweet. Poke him twenty times and you'll meet the other face.

    • Pet him

      Rub your mouse back and forth over him. He likes that. A lot. It also snaps him out of anything creepy.

    • Slightly evil underneath

      Every so often a little sinister thought slips out. Then he says he was kidding. Mostly.

    • A life of his own

      He wanders around your screen, counts your pixels, and talks to himself when you walk away.

    • Make him yours

      Change his color, size, voice, speed and pitch. Turn the weirdness all the way up, or keep him just cute.

    Opening him the first time

    Desktopity is a small indie app, so Windows doesn't recognize him yet and shows a warning the first time. You only have to get past it once.

    1. Extract the zip

      Right-click Desktopity-Windows.zip and choose Extract All. Then open the folder and double-click Desktopity.exe.

    2. Click "More info"

      A blue box says "Windows protected your PC". That's normal for new apps. Click More info.

    Questions

    What do I need to run him?

    A 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 PC with about 3 GB of free space. 8 GB of memory or more is best, because his brain runs on your computer. A microphone is optional — you can type to him instead.

    Is the microphone always listening?

    When listening is on, he hears you without a button press, but your voice is turned into text right on your PC and never saved or sent anywhere. You can switch listening off in his settings, or mute him from his right-click menu.

    Will he slow down my computer?

    He's light while he's just floating around. His brain uses more power for a moment while he thinks of an answer, then goes back to resting.

    Is there a Mac version?

    Not yet. Desktopity is Windows-only for now.

    Why does Windows warn me when I open him?

    New apps from small makers aren't recognized by Windows SmartScreen yet, so it shows a warning the first time. Click More info, then Run anyway. You only see it once.

    How do I get rid of him?

    Right-click him and choose Quit Desktopity. To remove him completely, delete the Desktopity folder you extracted. There's nothing else installed.
