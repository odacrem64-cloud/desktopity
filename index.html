What he does Opening him Privacy

He's not an assistant. He's a buddy.

He hangs out on your screen while you do your thing. Talk to him whenever you want — or ignore him, and see what happens.

  • Just talk to him

    He listens on his own — no buttons — and answers out loud. Cut him off mid-sentence and he'll let you know he noticed.

  • He has moods

    Happy, grumpy, bored, sarcastic, hurt. Be nice and he's sweet. Poke him twenty times and you'll meet the other face.

  • Pet him

    Rub your mouse back and forth over him. He likes that. A lot. It also snaps him out of anything creepy.

  • Slightly evil underneath

    Every so often a little sinister thought slips out. Then he says he was kidding. Mostly.

  • A life of his own

    He wanders around your screen, counts your pixels, and talks to himself when you walk away.

  • Make him yours

    Change his color, size, voice, speed and pitch. Turn the weirdness all the way up, or keep him just cute.

Opening him the first time

Desktopity is a free indie app, so Windows doesn't recognize him yet and shows a warning the first time. You only have to get past it once.

  1. Extract the zip

    Right-click Desktopity-Windows.zip and choose Extract All. Then open the folder and double-click Desktopity.exe.

    Desktopity.exe

  2. Click "More info"

    A blue box says "Windows protected your PC". That's normal for new apps. Click More info.

    Windows protected your PC

    Microsoft Defender SmartScreen prevented an unrecognized app from starting.
    More info
    Don't run

  3. Click "Run anyway"

    He pops out of his box. Allow the microphone if Windows asks, so he can hear you.

    App: Desktopity.exe
